How MTS Safeguards Every Bale of Tobacco

MTS is a traceability and quality-assurance system for tobacco leaving the processing floor. Every bale is registered against its declared weight and grade, automatically re-weighed at a checkpoint, and if the two figures disagree — flagged and set aside before it can proceed. This document sets out exactly how that assurance works: the process a bale moves through, the systems that carry it out, and the precise exchanges between them.

IV.

Message Protocol

The complete exchange of messages a single bale generates, from registration to the final alarm decision, in the order they occur. Expand any step below for further explanation.

Figure 3 · Message Sequence

Pan & zoom
1 · Bale is registered
The bale number, grade, truck, farm, farmer and declared weight are submitted to the server, which records the bale and returns confirmation.
2 · Checkpoint identifies the bale
The fixed reader (Antenna 1) reports the bale's identifier. The server marks it as identified and opens a short window in which to receive a weight.
3 · The scale reports weight
No request is made of the scale — it streams continuously. The server's monitoring process removes any residual weight left on the platform and matches a stable reading to the most recently identified bale.
4 · Server determines the outcome
The measured weight is compared with the declared weight against a fixed tolerance. The bale's final status — and, where applicable, a stated reason — is recorded.
5 · Downstream reader checks the outcome
A read-only enquiry is made of the server. This step cannot alter the bale's record, so it cannot interfere with the checkpoint's own process.
6 · Alarm decision
If the outcome was a discrepancy, the reader's alarm sounds briefly so the bale can be withdrawn. Any other outcome produces no response at all.
A note on reliability. A stationary bale is read repeatedly by an always-on antenna, roughly every three seconds. Two safeguards prevent this from repeatedly re-triggering the weighing process: the residual measurement is only updated while no bale is expected, and is held fixed the instant a new bale is identified — and once a bale has an outcome, a further reading within ten seconds is treated as the same bale still present, not a new arrival.
